# Literal and terminal-only NFLVR fail

Lean: `not_literal_nflvr`, `not_terminal_bdd_nflvr` (library, `SignatureVolatility/NflvrDoubling.lean`); compared as `not_literal_nflvr_unwrapped` and `not_terminal_bdd_nflvr_unwrapped`. The construction and the distinction among the three NFLVR wordings are due to Gemini "Carbon"; a verbatim excerpt of the outside audit is in [`appendix/carbon_nflvr.md`](appendix/carbon_nflvr.md).

The paper's Definition 2.17 (S-admissible strategies) asks only that a predictable $H$ satisfy $\int_0^T H_s^2 S_s^2 \sigma_s^2\,ds < \infty$ a.s., with no lower bound on the wealth $V^H$. Definition 4.3 then forms the cone of terminal gains of such strategies. Section 4.6 later writes that the terminal wealths are "bounded from below". Neither wording is the Delbaen–Schachermayer condition, which requires a **uniform-in-time** bound: some $a \ge 0$ with $V^H_t \ge -a$ a.s. for every $t \in [0,T]$.

Lean refutes the first two wordings, and only those, for the Black–Scholes market ($T > 0$, $s_0 > 0$, $\sigma_0 \neq 0$, one-dimensional Brownian motion), with price $S = \mathtt{sigPrice}$ of `blackScholesModel` (the closed-form $s_0 \exp(\sigma_0 B_t - \tfrac12 \sigma_0^2 t)$ on $[0,T]$) and constant volatility $\sigma_0$.

The counterexample is a geometric-grid doubling strategy, ported from Carbon's draft. On the grid $t_n = T(1-2^{-n})$, the integrand $K$ holds the constant weight $c_n = (1-X_{t_n})/\sqrt{\Delta t_n}$ on the stochastic interval $(t_n, \tau_n]$, where $\tau_n$ is the first hit of $B_t - B_{t_n} = \sqrt{\Delta t_n}$, and is zero after the first miss. No Dambis–Dubins–Schwarz time change and no reflection principle are used: the decay $P(E_n) \le q^n$ comes from independent Brownian increments. The wealth $V = \int K\,dB$ starts at $0$, has continuous paths, and satisfies $V_T = 1$ almost surely. $K$ is predictable and square-integrable on $[0,T]$ pathwise, hence S-admissible for $S = \sigma = 1$. The Black–Scholes transfer is $H = K/(S\sigma_0)$, which is S-admissible for the Black–Scholes price and satisfies $H S \sigma_0 = K$, so the same $V$ is the Itô integral in that market.

Thus $V_T = 1$ is a nontrivial free lunch in the literal cone (no lower bound) and also in the terminal-only bounded-below cone (since $V_T \ge 0$ a.s.). That does **not** refute Delbaen–Schachermayer NFLVR: this $V$ is unbounded below on $[0,T]$. The compared theorems do not claim that the uniformly bounded-below cone satisfies NFLVR. The NFLVR conjunct of `theorem_B_mp_unwrapped` still uses the older set `AdmissibleGainsUnderP`, not the Delbaen–Schachermayer cone.
