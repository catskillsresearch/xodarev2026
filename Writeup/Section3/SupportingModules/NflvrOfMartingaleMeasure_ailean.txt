**Theorem** (`nflvr_of_martingale_measure`). On a probability space `(Ω, ℙ)`, the admissible-gain class `AdmissibleGainsUnderP ℙ` satisfies the no-free-lunch-with-vanishing-risk condition `SatisfiesNFLVR ℙ (_)`.

Proof. Unfold `SatisfiesNFLVR` and consider an admissible integrand family `G`, a limiting integrand `f`, and the hypotheses: `f` is integrable, nonnegative almost surely, gains `G n` vanish from below at the rates prescribed by `hvanish`, each `G (subseq k)` is integrable with integral at most zero (`hG_int`), and `f` is the almost-sure limit of `G n` along the filter (`hae`). The proof shows `f` is almost surely zero.

Suppose for contradiction that `∫ f dℙ` is positive. Set `δ` to half that integral, so `0 < δ`. From `hvanish`, for each `k` there is a threshold `N_fn k` beyond which `G n ω ≥ -δ/(k+1)` for all `ω`. Choose a strictly increasing subsequence `subseq` that eventually dominates each `N_fn k`, verified monotone via `Nat.rec` and `le_max_left`. Define `H k ω = G (subseq k) ω + δ`. Nonnegativity of `H k` combines the vanishing lower bound at index `subseq k` with `δ/(k+1) ≤ δ`. Integrability of each `H k` adds the constant `δ` to the integrability of `G (subseq k)`. The Bochner integral satisfies `∫ H k ≤ δ` because the integral splits into that of `G (subseq k)` (nonpositive by `hG_int`) plus `δ`.

Pass to extended nonnegative reals: `H k` is almost-everywhere measurable, and Fatou’s lemma `lintegral_liminf_le'` applies. Almost surely, `ENNReal.ofReal (f + δ) ≤ liminf_k ENNReal.ofReal (H k ω)` because `f + δ` is the limit of `H k` along the subsequence composed with the convergence `hae`. Monotonicity of the lower integral gives `∫⁻ ofReal (f + δ) ≤ liminf_k ∫⁻ ofReal (H k)`. Each `∫⁻ ofReal (H k)` is at most `ENNReal.ofReal δ` by converting back with `ofReal_integral_eq_lintegral_ofReal` and `ENNReal.ofReal_le_ofReal` using `hH_int_le`. Hence the liminf is bounded by `ofReal δ`. On the left, `ofReal_integral_eq_lintegral_ofReal` identifies `∫⁻ ofReal (f + δ)` with `ofReal (∫ (f + δ))` using integrability and nonnegativity of `f + δ`. Chaining inequalities yields `∫ (f + δ) ≤ δ`, and splitting the integral contradicts `δ > 0`. Therefore `∫ f ≤ 0`.

With `f` integrable and nonnegative, `∫ f ≥ 0`, so `∫ f = 0`. By `integral_eq_zero_iff_of_nonneg_ae`, `f =ᵐ 0`. This is the NFLVR conclusion.
