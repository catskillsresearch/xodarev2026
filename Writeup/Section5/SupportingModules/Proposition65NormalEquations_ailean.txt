**Theorem** (`proposition_6_5_normal_equations`). Let `H` be a real inner product space, `𝒰 : Submodule ℝ H`, target `ε` and approximation `u_star ∈ 𝒰`. Suppose `u_star` satisfies the normal equations in the sense that `⟪ε - u_star, v⟫ = 0` for every `v ∈ 𝒰`. Then for every `u ∈ 𝒰`, the squared error is minimized at `u_star`: `‖ε - u_star‖² ≤ ‖ε - u‖²`, and the minimal squared error satisfies the Pythagorean identity `‖ε - u_star‖² = ‖ε‖² - ‖u_star‖²`.

Proof. For the optimality inequality, fix `u ∈ 𝒰`. Then `u_star - u ∈ 𝒰` by submodule closure under subtraction. Orthogonality gives `⟪ε - u_star, u_star - u⟫ = 0`. Write `ε - u = (ε - u_star) + (u_star - u)` by `abel`. The norm-square expansion `norm_add_sq_real` for orthogonal summands yields `‖ε - u‖² = ‖ε - u_star‖² + ‖u_star - u‖²`; nonnegativity of `‖u_star - u‖²` gives the desired inequality via `nlinarith`.

For the variance formula, orthogonality with `v = u_star` gives `⟪ε - u_star, u_star⟫ = 0`. Decompose `ε = (ε - u_star) + u_star`. Apply `norm_add_sq_real` to this orthogonal split; substituting the zero cross term leaves `‖ε‖² = ‖ε - u_star‖² + ‖u_star‖²`, and rearranging gives `‖ε - u_star‖² = ‖ε‖² - ‖u_star‖²`.
